# Claims map

Use this file when the reader asks what the essay claims or wants to inspect the evidence. Keep the claim list short so the reader can choose where to go deeper.

## Claim 1: AI progress is real

Models are getting better fast. Benchmarks and long-horizon task results should not be hand-waved away.

- **Best evidence:** benchmark progress such as GDPVal, Humanity's Last Exam, and METR time horizons.
- **Strongest unresolved question:** how much benchmark progress transfers to open-ended work where the task, success criteria, and stakes are not already cleanly framed?
- **Where to look:** `sources/public-sources.md`.

## Claim 2: Work with agents has two main shapes

Some agents act like employees: they live inside workflows, have jobs, and can be assigned tasks. The more important mode is human-agent collaboration inside tools like Codex, Claude Code, and Cowork, where the human and agent work in the same workspace.

- **Best evidence:** Every examples around Claudie, Andie, Viktor, Fin, Codex, Claude Code, Proof, and email workflows.
- **Strongest unresolved question:** which kinds of work belong with async employee-style agents, and which require live human-agent collaboration?
- **Where to look:** `after-automation.md`, `case-studies/every-ai-native-workflows.md`.

## Claim 3: Every agent needs a human

Agents need someone to point them at the right work, decide whether the output is good, catch what is wrong, and turn results into real decisions or processes. The further an agent gets from a responsible human owner, the worse it works.

- **Best evidence:** Every's shift away from one personal agent per employee and toward team or company-level agents with owners, maintenance, and review loops.
- **Strongest unresolved question:** how much of agent maintenance can itself be delegated to other agents before a human owner still has to step in?
- **Where to look:** `after-automation.md`, `case-studies/every-ai-native-workflows.md`, `prompts/objections-and-responses.md`.

## Claim 4: AI makes yesterday's competence cheap

Models are trained on the visible residue of past human work. That makes skills that used to be rare easier for many more people to attempt.

- **Best evidence:** Every examples where non-engineers file pull requests, marketers make creative assets, engineers draft guides, and support workflows route more work through agents.
- **Strongest unresolved question:** which workers and companies get leverage from this, and which ones get displaced or pushed into lower-quality work?
- **Where to look:** `case-studies/every-ai-native-workflows.md`, `sources/public-sources.md`.

## Claim 5: Cheap competence creates sameness, and sameness creates demand for difference

When many people use similar models trained on similar corpora, output tends to converge. Slop is visible sameness. As sameness spreads, valuable work moves toward specificity, judgment, and fit to the live situation.

- **Best evidence:** Every workflow examples where agent output needs human review, specificity, and a stronger quality bar before shipping.
- **Strongest unresolved question:** in which markets do people actually demand difference, and in which markets do they accept cheaper sameness?
- **Where to look:** `case-studies/every-ai-native-workflows.md`, `sources/public-sources.md`.

## Claim 6: Benchmarks measure work inside frames

A benchmark score measures how well a model performs inside a task, prompt, scoring rubric, and evaluation frame someone created. When a model saturates one frame, the human work often moves one level up.

- **Best evidence:** GDPVal task prompts, METR task definitions, and the essay's discussion of smuggled intelligence.
- **Strongest unresolved question:** how quickly will models learn to help choose, compare, and revise frames themselves?
- **Where to look:** `after-automation.md`, `sources/public-sources.md`.

## Claim 7: The frame is not the framer

Even if a model can choose and re-choose frames, it is still acting in pursuit of a goal, reward, or signal that someone decided should count as progress. The model can climb frames, but the framer is still in contact with the whole live situation the frame leaves out.

- **Best evidence:** the AGI section, the frame/framer distinction, and the objections about continuous learning, model-to-model prompting, and recursive improvement.
- **Strongest unresolved question:** what changes if future systems generate their own ends instead of optimizing goals humans supply?
- **Where to look:** `after-automation.md`, `prompts/objections-and-responses.md`.

## Claim 8: The useful comparison is human plus AI versus human plus AI

The essay argues that AI is not racing humans by itself. AI is inert until at least one human tells it where to go, and because AI is broadly distributed, the practical contest is between people and organizations using AI.

- **Best evidence:** the Zeno Redux section, the agents-without-agency section, and Every workflow examples.
- **Strongest unresolved question:** where does this stop being true if agents become persistent systems with goals of their own?
- **Where to look:** `after-automation.md`, `case-studies/every-ai-native-workflows.md`, `prompts/objections-and-responses.md`.
